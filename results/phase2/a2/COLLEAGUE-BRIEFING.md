# Before you start — please read this page

## What this is

This is a tool that teaches an AI assistant about a company's data. It
keeps a written, checked description of that data — what each table
holds, which numbers are trustworthy, how the pieces connect — and hands
that description to the assistant the moment you ask a question. The
point is that you should be able to ask for something in your own
words and get a real answer built from real data, with honest notes
attached about anything the system is unsure of.

## What you'll do

It should take under an hour, and most of it is you typing in plain
English.

1. **Get your setup.** Open the web address you were given. Sign in with
   the account that was created for you — it is *yours*, nobody else
   uses it. A small file downloads.
2. **Unpack it and open the assistant.** The step-by-step commands are
   on the sheet handed to you with this page. If the sheet does not
   work, that is exactly the sort of thing we want to hear about.
3. **Ask for a report.** Anything you actually want to know from this
   data. Not a script we wrote — your question, in your words. If you
   are not sure what to ask, ask the assistant what it knows about, and
   pick something from that.
4. **Follow the conversation honestly.** The assistant will ask you
   things: which numbers you mean, how you want them grouped, whether a
   definition matches what you had in mind. Answer with what you really
   think, not with what you guess it wants to hear. If it tells you it
   cannot do something, or that a document is unverified, take it at its
   word and see where the conversation goes.
5. **Stop when you have something, or when you are stuck.** Both are
   fine endings. Being stuck is a result, not a failure.

## The two rules

**1. The operator cannot help you.** They can hand you this page and the
command sheet, and that is all. If you ask them what a screen means,
they will tell you they are not allowed to answer. This is not rudeness
— the entire question we are testing is whether this works for someone
who is not the person who built it. Every time they would have leaned
over and explained something is a place the product needs to explain
itself.

**2. If something confuses you, say it out loud.** Literally out loud,
as it happens: "I don't know what this word means", "I expected that to
do something else", "I have no idea if that worked". Confusion is the
data we want. It is worth more than a smooth run. Nothing you say here
counts against you or against the tool — the moments where you paused
are the entire point of your being here.

## What is being watched

Not you. The product. Nobody is timing you, scoring your questions, or
comparing you to anyone. The operator is writing down where the thing
tripped you up, so it can stop doing that.

If at any point you want to stop, stop. That is also a result.

Thank you — genuinely. This half hour tells us things no amount of
internal testing can.
